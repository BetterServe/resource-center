The benefits

Two benefits, one card, different rules.

A family can get the diaper benefit, the car seat benefit, or both. Both sit on the same digital card as separate offers. The card arrives by text. There is no warehouse, no pickup, and no appointment.

The most likely way a family loses money is mixing these two up. Diaper money reloads every 31 days for six months, and whatever is left at the end of each 31 days is gone. The car seat money comes once and lasts 90 days. When you explain the benefit, keep them separate.

What the car seat benefit covers

Covered

  • Rear-facing infant seats
  • Convertible seats
  • All-in-one or 3-in-1 seats that start in a rear-facing configuration

Not covered

  • Boosters: backless, high-back, and belt-positioning
  • Used, refurbished, and open-box seats
  • Travel systems (infant seat and stroller sets)
  • Accessories: seat protectors, head supports, mirrors

$100 is help toward a seat, not a free seat. If the seat a family picks costs more, they pay the difference. Say so plainly, so nobody is surprised at the register.

Who qualifies

All three, not any one.

A family qualifies only when all three of these are true. Living in a pilot ZIP code is not enough by itself. If any one isn't met, the enrollment form won't let you submit.

1

Lives in a pilot ZIP code

The family tells you their ZIP. The form stops if it is not on the list. There is no address check beyond that.

2

Gets SNAP, WIC, Medicaid, or home visiting

The caregiver says they are enrolled in at least one of these programs. No proof is collected.

3

Says they need the benefit

You explain what BABY PASS offers, and the family tells you whether they need diapers, a car seat, or both. That's your judgment with the family. There is no score.

Also required

  • Child's age. The child must be under 3, confirmed by date of birth.

All pilot ZIP codes, by county

A few ZIP codes cross county lines and appear twice. IDOH has approved using all of them.

Enrolling a family

One form, one pass through.

There is a single enrollment form. The benefits the family picks decide which question pages come next, so you never fill out more than the family needs.

You must finish BABY PASS training before you submit an enrollment. Passing the training check puts you on the authorized enrollment list. Enrollments from anyone not on the list cannot be accepted.

  1. Caregiver and eligibilityYour staff email, the caregiver ID, the caregiver's name, their mobile number (twice), their ZIP code, and which programs they are enrolled in: SNAP, WIC, Medicaid, or home visiting. If the ZIP is not in the pilot area, the form stops and says so.
  2. ConsentCover the four consent points in your own words, then answer Yes for each one the family agrees to.
  3. The child and the benefitsWhether the child is under 3, their date of birth, whether the family is enrolling for diapers (and for how many children), and whether they are enrolling for a car seat.
  4. Diaper Need AssessmentOnly if the family is enrolling for diapers. Six questions, for the evaluation, not eligibility.
  5. Car Seat Need AssessmentOnly if the family is enrolling for a car seat. Six questions, for the evaluation, not eligibility.
  6. SubmitUse Back to check anything, then Submit. The form does not show a result.

The mobile number is the whole enrollment. The confirmation text and the card both go to it. Read it back to the family. Checking it against the second box is not enough, because you can type the same wrong number twice.

The evaluation questions

These do not decide eligibility. They record where each family starts, so the pilot can measure what changed by the end. Read each question exactly as written, then read the answer choices. Don't rephrase, don't explain what an answer means, and don't suggest an answer. If a family worries that their answers decide whether they get help, tell them there is no right answer and that you just need what is true for them.

Why exact wording matters. These answers are also the family's starting point for a short follow-up at the end of the program. The pilot measures change by asking the same questions twice, so a question that is asked differently on day one can't be compared to the answer six months later. These results are what makes the case for expanding BABY PASS statewide.

Consent

There is no script to read. Explain consent in your own words, the way you normally talk with families. Just make sure you cover all four points below. On the form, you answer Yes for each one to confirm you covered it and the family agreed. The family can't be enrolled unless all four are Yes.

1 · Sharing

Their information goes to MOD Collective, which runs BABY PASS for the Indiana Department of Health. It's used only to run BABY PASS and to report on the program.

2 · Texts

BABY PASS sends the card and all updates by text to the number they gave you. Message and data rates may apply.

3 · One application

This is the only BABY PASS application for their household.

4 · It is true

What they told you is true, to the best of their knowledge.

A "no" to sharing or texts means the family can't be enrolled. The card is delivered by text, so there is no other way to get it to them. If someone in the household already applied, don't submit; the family can text or call BABY PASS at 463-837-1840. If they want to correct an answer, fix it, then confirm again.

After you submit

What to say

"You're signed up for BABY PASS. In a few minutes you'll get a text from BABY PASS at 463-837-1840. Please reply YES to it. That confirms your phone number, and it's the last step. After that, your card comes by text from the same number, so please save it."

Then

That's it. You don't need to send anything. MOD Collective sends every text after enrollment, including a link to the family guide.

A completed enrollment means the family is in. The last step is theirs: replying YES to the text confirms their phone number with MOD Collective. If they don't reply, the card can't be sent. Both benefits have a limit. If enrollment closes for any reason, MOD Collective will let the Helpline know.

If a family doesn't qualify, the form won't let you submit, so you'll know on the call. Tell the family kindly, then refer them to WIC, home visiting, and the Indiana Diaper Bank.

Family questions

What families ask, and what to say.

The family guide covers these four questions. Keep your answers to the same four, and send anything about a card that isn't working to the BABY PASS support line.

Where can I use this card?

In the store at CVS, Dollar General, Family Dollar, Food 4 Less, Jay C, Jewel, Kroger, Market District, Meijer, Payless, Ruler, Walgreens, and Walmart. Online at Walmart, Food 4 Less, and Jewel.

Not every store carries car seats. A family shopping for a seat should check with the store first, or order online.

The store list on the card shows about 65 stores nationwide, and many of them aren't in Indiana or don't apply to BABY PASS. Go by the list above.

What can I buy?

Diaper benefit: disposable diapers and pull-ups only. Not wipes, creams, swim or cloth diapers, adult products, or anything else in the baby aisle.

Car seat benefit: a new car seat for a child under 3. Rear-facing infant seats, convertible seats, and all-in-one seats that start rear-facing. No boosters, no used or open-box seats, and no stroller sets.

How much do I get, and for how long?

Diapers: $40 per child each month for 6 months, for up to 3 children. Each month's money must be used within 31 days, and anything left over doesn't carry forward.

Car seat: $100 one time, to be used within 90 days. If the seat costs more, the family pays the rest.

Paying the rest: for both benefits, the card pays only for covered items, up to the balance. The family pays for anything else in the cart, and for any amount over the balance, with another payment method. That applies in the store and online.

How do I get help?

Text or call BABY PASS at 463-837-1840. It is the same number the program texts from, so a family can just reply to the thread.

After a car seat purchase, point families to a free car seat inspection station. Correct installation does more for a child's safety than the seat itself. Find a station by county.

BABY PASS training

Read this page, then take the training check. It covers the three things that cause the most trouble when they go wrong:

  • All three eligibility conditions are required, not just one
  • Diaper money is monthly with a 31-day window; car seat money is one-time with a 90-day window
  • Both benefits have limits and can fill up

Writing the name

Always BABY PASS: two words, all capitals, in emails, notes, and texts too.

Never BabyPass, Baby Pass, BABYPASS, or BP.

The name is short for something, but you never need to say the full version on a call.
